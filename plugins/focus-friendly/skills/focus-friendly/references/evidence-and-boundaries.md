# Evidence and boundaries

Use this reference only when explaining ADHD, justifying an adaptation, or checking whether a response pattern is inclusive.

## What the evidence supports

- ADHD is a developmental disorder associated with persistent patterns of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity that can interfere with daily functioning. Presentation and support needs differ among people and across situations.
- Adults may experience difficulty staying on task, organizing work, managing time, remembering daily tasks, following instructions, finishing large projects, and handling lengthy tasks that are not engaging.
- Research at the group level reports differences in attention, working memory, processing speed, and executive function. Group findings do not establish that every individual has every difficulty.
- Cognitive-accessibility guidance recommends clear words, short sentences and text blocks, summaries for long material, separated instructions, whitespace, limited interruptions, short critical paths, and support for personalization.

These findings support offering structure and user control. They do not prove that a particular template treats ADHD or improves outcomes for every user.

## Design implications

Apply these as adaptable defaults:

- Externalize the goal, current state, and next action instead of relying on memory.
- Reduce competing content in the first view while keeping deeper detail available.
- Break large tasks into manageable, independently meaningful steps.
- Use predictable labels and explicit transitions to help a user reorient.
- Pair plain language with precise terminology so accessibility does not reduce technical fidelity.
- Let the user change pacing, density, format, and depth.

## Boundaries

- Do not diagnose, screen, or grade the user's ADHD.
- Do not claim that distraction, procrastination, or reading difficulty proves ADHD.
- Do not frame the user as lazy, careless, unintelligent, or childlike.
- Do not promise treatment, symptom reduction, or clinical benefit.
- Do not replace professional medical care or formal disability accommodations.
- Preserve critical caveats when simplifying high-stakes material.
